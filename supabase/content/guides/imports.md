**Check before you pay:** figures are indicative, as compiled in September 2026. Shipping rates, port charges and exchange rates change, so get written quotes from your supplier and a licensed clearing agent.

Zambia is landlocked, so every imported vehicle travels through a neighbouring country's port or road network before it reaches a Zambian border post.

## Buying from Japan
Japan is the main source of used passenger cars in Zambia.
- **Right-hand drive only.** Standard civilian passenger vehicles imported into Zambia must be right-hand drive (RHD). Left-hand-drive passenger vehicles are generally restricted from normal import and registration.
- **Pre-export inspection is compulsory.** Before shipping, the vehicle must pass an inspection under Zambian standard **ZS 560**, carried out by an authorised firm such as **JEVIC** (Japan Export Vehicle Inspection Center) or **QISJ**. The inspection checks the structure, mechanical systems, brakes, tyres and whether the odometer reading is genuine. It costs about **USD 350** (around K2,500 to K3,110).
- **Skipping the inspection is expensive.** A vehicle that arrives without a valid pre-export roadworthiness certificate attracts a penalty of **15% of its CIF value** (cost, insurance and freight) from ZRA and ZCSA, plus local inspection delays. On a USD 10,000 CIF vehicle that is USD 1,500 (about K41,250).
- **Shipping.** Cars travel on Roll-on/Roll-off (RoRo) ships from ports such as Yokohama, Nagoya or Kobe. The voyage to Southern Africa takes about 4 to 7 weeks.

## Buying from South Africa
Vehicles from South Africa are usually driven or carried on a car-carrier by road through Zimbabwe or Botswana.
- The vehicle needs an export clearance from the **South African Police Service (SAPS)**, which checks the engine and chassis numbers against the SARPCCO regional police database. This confirms the vehicle is free of finance, theft records and fraudulent paperwork.

It then enters Zambia through one of two One-Stop Border Posts:
- **Chirundu OSBP**, on the Zambezi River, for traffic coming through Zimbabwe.
- **Kazungula OSBP**, the road-rail bridge from Botswana, which avoids transit through Zimbabwe.

## Transit corridors compared
| | Japan via Dar es Salaam | Japan via Durban | South Africa by road |
|---|---|---|---|
| Typical vehicles | Passenger cars, sedans, SUVs, small buses | Heavy trucks, machinery, luxury SUVs | Commercial fleets, semi-trailers, regional passenger cars |
| Sea transit | 4 – 6 weeks | 5 – 7 weeks | None (road only) |
| Zambian border | Nakonde | Chirundu or Kazungula | Kazungula or Chirundu |
| Pre-export check | JEVIC or QISJ (ZS 560) | JEVIC or QISJ (ZS 560) | SAPS police clearance and SARPCCO check |
| Transit guarantee | TRA transit bond in Tanzania | SARS transit clearance or bond | SADC transit documents or customs guarantee |
| Typical port or border fees | About USD 400 (~K7,500) | About USD 500 | Varies |

### Dar es Salaam (Tanzania)
The main gateway for passenger cars. A Tanzanian clearing agent lodges a **TRA transit bond**, which guarantees the car will pass through Tanzania without being sold there. You pay a small bond premium rather than Tanzanian import duty. The car is then carried by truck, or driven on temporary transit plates, to the **Tunduma–Nakonde** border.

### Durban (South Africa)
Often used for heavy commercial trucks, machinery and luxury SUVs. The vehicle crosses South Africa under bond and enters Zambia through the southern border posts.

### Walvis Bay (Namibia)
Connected by the Trans-Kalahari corridor. The vehicle enters western Zambia at **Katima Mulilo / Sesheke**.

## Hidden costs to plan for
- **Port storage (demurrage).** Dar es Salaam gives about 7 days of free storage after the ship unloads. If paperwork is late, for example a delayed original Bill of Lading or slow transit-bond processing, charges of about **USD 20 to 50 per day** follow.
- **Agent acquittal fee.** Clearing agents typically charge around **K300** to acquit the bond in ZRA's ASYCUDA system once the vehicle has crossed the border.
- **Uninspected vehicles.** The 15% CIF penalty described above.

## Tips
- Make sure the JEVIC or QISJ inspection is done and recorded on the commercial invoice before the ship leaves Japan.
- Send your original documents (Bill of Lading, Japanese export certificate, TPIN and commercial invoice) to a licensed clearing agent **at least 10 days before the ship arrives**, so the transit bond is ready and you avoid storage charges.
- Passenger cars from Japan are usually best routed through Dar es Salaam or Walvis Bay. Trucks from South Africa can avoid Zimbabwean transit by using Kazungula.
